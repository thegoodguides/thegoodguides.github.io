Our Philosophy

The keys are in their hands

Young people already run the culture. Nobody handed them the music, the businesses, the fashion, or the art — they built all of it themselves. The work in their own neighbourhoods should be theirs to run, too.

There's a difference between a seat at the table and the keys to the house. A seat means young people are invited in — heard, on someone else's terms. The Good Guides was built to hand over the whole house: young people here lead the work, set the direction, and make the decisions, with mentors from their own neighbourhoods supporting them as they do. And in this house, everyone is a productive piece of the whole — no guests, no passengers: the young people, the mentors, the community around them, each one carrying part of the work.

Along the way, they pick up skills that stay with them long after any single program ends.

About Us

Built in Scarborough. Led by the youth it serves.

The Good Guides is a grassroots, Black-led non-profit in Scarborough. Here, inner-city youth run the programs and events in their own neighbourhoods — with experienced mentors behind them.

Where It Started

Built by the community, because the community had to

The Good Guides began in Scarborough in 2020, and has mentored more than 10,000 young people here since. The idea started with Swabir Shariff, who grew up in Toronto Community Housing there — and friends who grew up alongside him were part of it from the start. Together they had watched the services meant to help young people miss them entirely.

It wasn't a lack of money or good intentions. The workers came from somewhere else. The programs were designed somewhere else. Kids couldn't see themselves in any of it — nobody in the room knew their streets, their schools, or their lives, so nobody in the room was trusted.

So they built their own: mentors from those same neighbourhoods, people young people could recognize as their own. It still runs the way it grew — mentors showing up week after week, and young people stepping up to lead alongside them.

Why We Exist

Our vision & mission

Our Vision

A generation of young people in Scarborough who lead in their own neighbourhoods — building skills, running programs, and making their streets stronger, with mentors behind them.

Our Mission

We mentor youth in Scarborough and work with their families, week after week, as young people grow into leaders — through after-school mentoring, violence-prevention workshops, and co-op placements where Ambassadors learn to lead.

What We Stand For

Our core values

Inclusivity

Every young person belongs here.

Respect

A caring space where young people are treated with respect — and expected to show it: to themselves, to others, and to their community.

Empowerment

Mentoring that puts youth and their families in charge of the decisions that shape their lives.

Creativity

Communities keep changing, so we keep adjusting — trying new ideas and keeping what works.

Our Philosophy

Led by youth, backed by adults

Adults here have a specific job: show up every week, bring the experience and the resources, and stand behind the young people running the work. Youth set the direction. We make sure they have what they need to carry it through — and what their work earns goes back into the neighbourhoods it came from.

Good Questions

Frequently asked questions

Are you a registered charity? Can I get a tax receipt for my donation?

We're an incorporated non-profit in Ontario — not a registered charity — so we can't give you a tax receipt for your donation.

Your contribution still goes straight into the weekly work: after-school mentoring and virtual hangouts, violence prevention workshops, hot meals delivered to neighbours, and excursion days for youth.

How do I donate?

Donations run through Donorbox. Tap any Donate button on this site and you'll land on our secure donation page — one-time and monthly gifts are both welcome, and monthly donors keep the weekly work going.

Donate through Donorbox

Can I donate goods instead of money?

Yes — in-kind gifts go straight to work: food for the serving line, sports gear, backpacks and school supplies in September, and gifts and treat bags through the holidays. Goods come to us through the partnership route — start with the partnership form, or see Partners for the ways in. Money donations run through Donorbox — see "How do I donate?" above.

How do I volunteer, become a mentor, or become an Ambassador?

Start with the volunteer sign-up form and we'll be in touch. Our volunteers mentor youth after school and help run youth-created events — showing up week after week is the heart of it.

Students: you can start with a co-op placement and earn school credits as a Good Guide Ambassador. See Our Programs for how placements work.

What ages do you work with?

Our programs are for young people ages 11–18, after the school day ends, in the Scarborough neighbourhoods they call home — plus virtual hangouts between sessions. There is no long application; joining starts with a conversation.

How do I refer a young person?

Teachers, family members, and workers can all refer a young person. Start through our Contact page — it routes parents, students, and teachers to the right person — and tell us the young person's neighbourhood. There is no long referral form; joining starts with a conversation.

Where are you based?

Our office is at 2229 Kingston Rd Unit B, Scarborough, ON M1N 1T8, and our programs run in neighbourhoods across Scarborough.

Can you visit our school? Do you work outside Scarborough?

We visit schools and community centres across Scarborough, and we build workshops with our partners there. Our work is Scarborough-based — the neighbourhoods our youth call home — and that is where our programs run.

How can my organization partner with The Good Guides?

Start with the partnership form and tell us what you have in mind. Partners build workshops with us, host co-op students, back event days, and put contributions and in-kind gifts to work for Scarborough youth — see Partners for the ways in.

How do I reach you?

Email us at service@thegoodguides.org, or send a message through the form on our Contact page — parents, students, teachers, and businesses will each find the right route there.

How It Works

It starts with listening

Everything starts with working with the youth to hear their needs and wants. And we go from there.

  1. 1

    Listen first

    Before anything is planned, we work with the youth to hear their needs and wants.

    They know their streets, their schools, and their lives — so what gets built begins with what they say they need. Nothing here is designed somewhere else and handed down.

  2. 2

    The youth build from there

    Ambassadors plan and run what the listening produces — with experienced mentors and the full backing of the organization behind them.

    Programs & workshops

    Ambassadors created the virtual hangouts that keep young people in touch with their mentors between sessions — and they run them. This step also carries our workshops on preventing violence and settling conflict, delivered across Scarborough — workshops the City of Toronto has funded since 2023. Young people pick up résumé writing, financial literacy, CPR & first aid, and job prep through the work itself.

    Events

    Ambassadors created and hosted résumé nights — young people left with a finished résumé — bank-account sign-up events, where youth opened their own accounts, and OSAP sessions that explained how student funding works. With community partners, they also ran Funday With Cops and the Toronto Sports Festival, created Community Graduation 2K21, and helped at Woburn Basketball Day, which was organized by the Toronto Police Service.

    Trips & excursions

    Ambassadors don't just go on trips — they choose the destination, plan the day, and run it themselves. At Brooks Farms in Mount Albert (September 2020), youth spent a day on a working farm — hiking, zip-lining, and apple picking — while single parents got a day of relief back home. Ice fishing trips run the same way: chosen, planned, and led by Ambassadors.

  3. 3

    The wider community feels it

    The work doesn't stop at the youth in the room — neighbours, families, and schools feel it where they live.

    Ambassadors do this work for the whole community, not just the youth in the room: hot meals carried to neighbours living in encampments, gifts and treat bags handed out close to home, a day of relief for single parents on excursion days, and a Community Resource Fair connecting families to services in their own neighbourhoods.

Partner With Us

Ways to partner

Build a program with us

Our workshops are designed with the partners who deliver them — violence-prevention sessions with the Toronto Police Service's Community Partnership and Engagement Unit. If your organization runs something Scarborough youth should have, let's design the session together.

Take a co-op student

Scarborough schools place co-op students with us, and their teachers stay in the loop from first day to final evaluation. If your school or workplace can host a placement, a young person gets real hours, real work, and a real reference.

Stand behind an event

Our biggest days are shared days. Funday With Cops and the Toronto Sports Festival run with partner organizations, and the Community Resource Fair puts services in one room for families. Partners bring people, gear, food, and space — the day gets bigger for every one that shows up.

Fund a program day

Program days run on contributions — the barbecue food, the trip buses, the room for graduation. Funding a day, a season, or a single trip puts your support inside a moment the youth will remember, with the Ambassadors running it end to end.

Give what's in your hands

In-kind gifts go straight to work: food for the serving line, sports gear, backpacks and school supplies in September, gifts and treat bags through the holidays. If your business makes it, stocks it, or has it on a shelf, our youth can use it.

Lend your profession

Some of our strongest sessions are taught by people who do the work for a living — résumé nights, first bank accounts, CPR and first aid, money basics, OSAP walkthroughs. Professionals back the organization itself, too, with space, skills, and advice. One hour of what you know can change what a young person believes is possible.
