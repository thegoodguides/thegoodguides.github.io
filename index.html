Our Philosophy

The keys are in their hands

Young people already run the culture. Nobody handed them the music, the businesses, the fashion, or the art — they took control of all of it themselves. They deserve that same control over what heals their own neighbourhoods.

So here, youth stand on the front lines of repairing their communities — building the programs, running the events, and shaping the supports their streets actually need. And holding the keys never means going it alone: behind every young leader stands real support, starting with mentors from their own neighbourhoods — people young people can actually see themselves in. Along the way, they build real skills that stay with them long after any single program ends.

We empower, educate, and encourage — but encouragement was never the job. The point is that they BECOME the change their communities have been waiting for.

Where It Started

Built by the community, because the community had to

The Good Guides was founded in Scarborough in 2020 by Swabir Shariff, in the Toronto Community Housing neighbourhoods he grew up in.

He had watched the services meant to help young people in those neighbourhoods fail to reach them — not for lack of funding or good intentions, but because the kids couldn't see themselves in any of it. The workers came from somewhere else. The programs were designed somewhere else. Nobody in the room knew their streets, their schools, or their lives — so nobody in the room was trusted.

So the community built its own, staffed by mentors from those same neighbourhoods — people young people could recognize as their own. It grew the way it still runs today: those mentors showing up week after week, and young people stepping up to lead alongside them.

Our Philosophy

Led by youth, backed by adults

Nobody understands what young people face better than young people themselves — so here, young people don't just advise the work. They run it: building the programs, running the events, and shaping the supports their neighbourhoods need.

Running it, though, is not the same as being left on your own. Every young leader at The Good Guides works with the organization behind them — experienced mentors, real resources, and adults who show up week after week. Youth set the direction; we make sure they have what they need to carry it through.

That's how the work actually runs — and the resources those efforts bring flow straight back into the community they came from. Young people leave with real skills, and proof of what they can do.

Our Programs

Not just a chair at the table — the keys to the entire program.

The Good Guides doesn't invite youth in to watch. Young people create the programs, run the events, and shape the supports — for youth, and for the community around them.

And they never build alone: every young leader has mentors and the full backing of the organization behind them. Control in their hands, support at their backs — everything on this page runs on that premise.

The Core Program

Co-op & after-school: the engine everything runs on

Our core program runs two ways: co-op placements for students, and after-school programming for youth ages 11–18. It's where Good Guide Ambassadors come from — and it's the starting point for every event, workshop, and support on this page.

Co-op & Volunteer Pathway

Co-op Placements — Good Guide Ambassadors

We take co-op and placement students from high schools and post-secondary schools. They join the team as Good Guide Ambassadors for their own neighbourhoods — earning credits toward graduation while they plan and run real initiatives and events back home.

From there, Ambassadors plan and run real work — the workshops, events, and supports below. Placements can earn volunteer hours and school credits. If you're a student — or a teacher placing one — get in touch.

Ages 11–18

After-School Programming

After-school programming for young people ages 11–18 — mentoring and support after the school day ends, in the neighbourhoods our youth call home, including Eglinton East, Woburn, and Cedarbrae, plus virtual hangouts that keep them connected to their mentors and each other between sessions.

New faces are welcome, and joining is free — just come as you are, and we'll take it from there. Parents and youth: see "For Youth & Parents" below, and we'll match you with what's running near you.

How It Works

Three strands

Everything Ambassadors build falls into three strands:

Programs

Ambassadors created and run the virtual hangouts that keep young people connected to their mentors between sessions. This strand also carries the violence prevention workshops and de-escalation programming delivered in Eglinton East, Woburn, and Cedarbrae — The Good Guides is a City of Toronto Youth Violence Prevention Grant recipient. And the learning happens by doing: résumé writing, financial literacy, CPR & first aid, and job prep are skills young people pick up through the work itself.

Events

These events were hosted and created by our youth: résumé creation events that send young people out with a finished résumé, events run with banks so youth could open their own bank accounts, and sessions with OSAP so youth understand how student funding works. Youth also organized Funday With Cops and the Toronto Sports Festival together with community partners, created Community Graduation 2K21, and supported Woburn Basketball Day. Next up: Youth Sports Day on January 30, 2027 — a free sports day and shoe fitting for 300 Scarborough middle-school students.

Supports

Supports built for the whole community, not just the youth in the room — and identified, organized, and delivered by our youth themselves: hot-meal delivery and support for local homeless encampments, gifts and treat bags handed out in the community, relief for single parents on excursion days so their kids never miss out, and a Community Resource Fair connecting families to the help around them.

The Wider Record

What runs on the core

Built on the co-op and after-school core: City-funded violence prevention, community engagement, and the excursions Ambassadors plan and run themselves.

City of Toronto Grant Recipient

Youth Violence Prevention

The Good Guides is a recipient of the City of Toronto's Youth Violence Prevention Grant (2023), delivering violence prevention workshops and de-escalation programming for youth in Scarborough — including Eglinton East, Woburn, and Cedarbrae.

Community Engagement

Being present means showing up where Scarborough lives. Good Guides youth have played sports days alongside neighbourhood officers, cleared a truckload of garbage from the Doris McCarthy Trail with 43 Division and more than 25 community volunteers, celebrated graduations for students of every grade, and delivered hot meals to neighbours in encampments. That presence — repeated, reliable, in person — is how trust gets built here.

Perspective Excursions

Ambassadors don't just go on trips — they find the excursion, map out the steps, and execute it themselves. At Brooks Farms in Mount Albert (September 2020), youth spent a first day on a working farm — hiking, zip-lining, and apple picking — while The Good Guides provided relief for single parents back home. Ambassadors have also taken the model out on the ice with ice fishing trips. That's how a young person learns, by doing, that where they come from doesn't decide how far they go.
